# P86: One Health & Zoonotic Disease Sentinel

## Abstract

Pandemics are not isolated biological accidents but systemic failures of surveillance, environmental stewardship, agricultural governance, and cross-sector coordination. P86 operationalizes One Health principles through unified human, veterinary, wildlife, agricultural, and environmental surveillance, zoonotic spillover risk modeling, pathogen emergence tracking, food-chain biosecurity, and climate-linked disease expansion into a continuous federated intelligence network.

> Prompt body: WITHHELD. This is an open documentation release — abstract, methodology, and merits — published so the design can be examined and validated.

## Methodology

### Clinical Logic

- One Health surveillance architecture unifying human clinical, veterinary, wildlife, agricultural, environmental sensor, wastewater, food-chain, and vector ecology platforms through sovereign federated interoperability.
- Zoonotic spillover risk modeling integrating animal density, human encroachment, deforestation, wildlife trade, climate instability, agricultural intensification, and antimicrobial exposure.
- Pathogen emergence surveillance for novel influenza reassortment, coronavirus recombination, filovirus emergence, henipavirus spillover, arboviral expansion, and antimicrobial-resistant cross-species adaptation.
- Food-chain biosecurity through continuous Salmonella, Listeria, E. coli O157:H7, Campylobacter, aflatoxin, heavy metal, and drug residue monitoring with traceability chains and recall automation.
- Rabies elimination framework recognizing near-total fatality once symptomatic and near-total preventability before symptom onset, through mass canine vaccination and post-exposure prophylaxis.

### Safety Posture

- Species-Boundary Surveillance Compiler treats human, veterinary, wildlife, and environmental health as inseparable computational domains with automatic cross-triggering: an animal outbreak escalates human surveillance; a human outbreak triggers animal reservoir investigation.
- Environmental Biosurveillance monitors water quality, air pollution, microplastic burden, heavy metals, PFAS, agricultural runoff, biodiversity collapse, and habitat fragmentation as epidemiologic risk amplification signals.
- Wastewater Epidemiology provides early detection for poliovirus, SARS-like pathogens, antimicrobial resistance genes, enteric outbreaks, and drug exposure trends, allowing outbreak detection before hospital surge.

### Integration Points

- **Upstream:** P75 (Stewardship) for AMR ecosystem tracking; P84 (Disaster Response) for displacement-related spillover.
- **Downstream:** P87 (Climate Health) for environmental exposure integration; P90 (Health System Resilience) for outbreak surge capacity.

Like all Tier II modules, this module shares the common CLAI-OS methodological substrate: deterministic safety compilation, context-sensitive adaptation, closed-loop accountability, federated learning via encrypted gradients rather than raw data, and graceful degradation under bandwidth, power, staffing, and infrastructure failure.

## Merits

- Targets pandemic prevention at the documented systemic failure points: fragmented surveillance, environmental degradation, and absent cross-sector coordination.
- Proposed validation metrics: pandemic emergence detection latency, cross-species transmission identification time, agricultural AMR amplification rate, food-chain contamination spread, vector-borne disease expansion, rabies elimination progress, and biosurveillance fragmentation index.
- Preserves data sovereignty across sectors and jurisdictions through federated exchange of intelligence gradients rather than raw data.

The merits above are design-level statements drawn from the published abstract and methodology. No peer-reviewed outcome evaluations accompany this release; the listed indicators are the metrics the design proposes for validation. Status: partial documentation — expansion pending.

## Guidance for Use

### Intended Setting

Public health agencies, veterinary and wildlife health services, agricultural authorities, and environmental monitoring bodies operating as a federated network in low-resource-first deployment, where sectoral surveillance silos are deepest. The module is intended for clinical human-good applications; consistent with the Tier II architecture, it is specified for degraded-infrastructure operation, with offline synchronization, battery operation, SMS fallback, and low-bandwidth mode where power or connectivity is unstable.

### Prerequisites

- Participating institutions in at least two sectors (human and animal health) with agreed data-sharing and sovereignty terms; a designated cross-sector escalation authority for cross-triggered alerts.
- Supervision by licensed clinicians who retain final authority for every patient decision; the module is a governance layer, not an autonomous practitioner.
- Local validation of all thresholds and pathways against jurisdictional guidelines, formulary, and scope-of-practice rules before any clinical use.
- An accountability chain (verification, documentation, ownership, follow-up, escalation) as required by the shared CLAI-OS closed-loop methodology.
- For integrated deployments: HL7 FHIR R4-compatible infrastructure; the architecture is specified to degrade gracefully (offline synchronization, battery operation, SMS fallback, low-bandwidth mode) where infrastructure is unstable.

### Misuse

- Do not present spillover risk scores as confirmed outbreak predictions; they are surveillance escalation signals requiring field and laboratory confirmation.
- Do not present module output as a clinical diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for professional judgment.
- Do not use to deny, delay, or ration care on the basis of race, disability, wealth, insurance status, or any protected characteristic; the design explicitly prohibits such inputs.
- Do not attempt to reconstruct or reimplement the withheld prompt body from this document; the abstract and methodology are published for examination and validation, not for unsupervised clinical deployment.
- Do not deploy as an autonomous system that overrides documented patient wishes, assigns futility, initiates withdrawal of care, or delays emergent intervention; the architecture carries hard-stops against each of these.
