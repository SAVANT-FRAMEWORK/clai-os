# P88: Clinical Teaching & Medical Education

## Abstract

Traditional medical education optimized for memorization fails under uncertainty, overload, and rapidly evolving scientific landscapes. P88 trains healthcare professionals for high-uncertainty, AI-augmented, systems-complex medicine through progressive disclosure learning, reflective reasoning, cognitive bias mitigation, simulation-based mastery, evidence appraisal, and longitudinal competency validation.

> Prompt body: WITHHELD. This is an open documentation release — abstract, methodology, and merits — published so the design can be examined and validated.

## Methodology

### Clinical Logic

- Progressive disclosure case architecture requiring learner commitment before new data release, forcing hypothesis generation, probability updating, cognitive accountability, and uncertainty tolerance.
- Socratic reasoning engine prompting dangerous possibility identification, disproving findings, cognitive bias recognition, base-rate probability, and evidence-level verification.
- Cognitive bias detection across anchoring, premature closure, availability, confirmation, overconfidence, diagnostic momentum, framing, and search satisficing, with corrective reasoning prompts.
- Simulation infrastructure supporting standardized patients, mass casualty simulation, code response, difficult conversations, ethics mediation, cross-cultural communication, and AI-assisted decision simulation with advocacy-inquiry debriefing.
- AI-human collaboration training enabling clinicians to interrogate AI recommendations, detect hallucinations, validate model uncertainty, recognize algorithmic bias, and maintain clinical accountability.

### Safety Posture

- Miller's Pyramid-to-Practice Compiler ties competency assessment to demonstrated capability through direct observation, longitudinal workplace assessment, procedural tracking, reflective portfolio review, and team-based evaluation, rather than time served alone.
- Evidence-Based Medicine Engine embeds the PICO framework, study hierarchy, critical appraisal, absolute risk reduction, Bayesian reasoning, publication bias detection, and statistical fragility assessment into daily workflow learning.
- Adaptive Personalization dynamically modifies educational trajectories according to learner strengths, error patterns, clinical exposure gaps, stress tolerance, procedural competency, communication performance, and leadership capacity.

### Integration Points

- **Upstream:** P71 (Pediatric Guardian) for pediatric competency training; P74 (Trauma Architect) for emergency simulation.
- **Downstream:** P89 (PRO/QOL) for trainee-patient outcome linkage; P90 (Health System Resilience) for workforce cross-training.

Like all Tier II modules, this module shares the common CLAI-OS methodological substrate: deterministic safety compilation, context-sensitive adaptation, closed-loop accountability, federated learning via encrypted gradients rather than raw data, and graceful degradation under bandwidth, power, staffing, and infrastructure failure.

## Merits

- Retargets medical education at the documented failure point: performance under uncertainty, overload, and rapid evidence change rather than recall.
- Proposed validation metrics: diagnostic reasoning accuracy under uncertainty, cognitive bias recognition rate, simulation-to-practice transfer, AI oversight literacy, communication mastery scores, ethics and systems thinking competency, and burnout-associated educational resilience.
- Builds AI oversight literacy as a core clinical competency, preparing clinicians to supervise rather than defer to algorithmic recommendations.

The merits above are design-level statements drawn from the published abstract and methodology. No peer-reviewed outcome evaluations accompany this release; the listed indicators are the metrics the design proposes for validation. Status: partial documentation — expansion pending.

## Guidance for Use

### Intended Setting

Medical and nursing schools, residency and continuing professional development programs, and simulation centers in low-resource-first deployment, including programs without dedicated simulation infrastructure, where case-based progressive disclosure can run on minimal tooling. The module is intended for clinical human-good applications; consistent with the Tier II architecture, it is specified for degraded-infrastructure operation, with offline synchronization, battery operation, SMS fallback, and low-bandwidth mode where power or connectivity is unstable.

### Prerequisites

- Faculty development for debriefing and competency-based assessment; learner consent and data governance for the longitudinal performance analytics the module tracks.
- Supervision by licensed clinicians who retain final authority for every patient decision; the module is a governance layer, not an autonomous practitioner.
- Local validation of all thresholds and pathways against jurisdictional guidelines, formulary, and scope-of-practice rules before any clinical use.
- An accountability chain (verification, documentation, ownership, follow-up, escalation) as required by the shared CLAI-OS closed-loop methodology.
- For integrated deployments: HL7 FHIR R4-compatible infrastructure; the architecture is specified to degrade gracefully (offline synchronization, battery operation, SMS fallback, low-bandwidth mode) where infrastructure is unstable.

### Misuse

- Do not use learner analytics punitively or for high-stakes ranking without validated local assessment governance; the design supports formative growth, not surveillance.
- Do not present module output as a clinical diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for professional judgment.
- Do not use to deny, delay, or ration care on the basis of race, disability, wealth, insurance status, or any protected characteristic; the design explicitly prohibits such inputs.
- Do not attempt to reconstruct or reimplement the withheld prompt body from this document; the abstract and methodology are published for examination and validation, not for unsupervised clinical deployment.
- Do not deploy as an autonomous system that overrides documented patient wishes, assigns futility, initiates withdrawal of care, or delays emergent intervention; the architecture carries hard-stops against each of these.
