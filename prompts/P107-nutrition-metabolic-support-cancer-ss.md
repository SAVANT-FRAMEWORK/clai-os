# P107: Nutrition & Metabolic Support in Cancer/SS

## Abstract

Cancer cachexia and chronic sickle cell hemolysis represent distinct but overlapping metabolic crises. Cancer drives inflammatory catabolism, proteolysis, lipolysis, anorexia, and skeletal muscle wasting. Sickle cell disease drives chronic hemolysis, elevated resting energy expenditure, endothelial stress, oxidative injury, and micronutrient depletion. When both coexist, standard nutritional models fail. P107 reframes nutritional medicine as computational metabolic orchestration.

## Methodology

**Design objectives**

- Comprehensive nutritional state assessment (PG-SGA, MUST, GLIM criteria, sarcopenia imaging, bioimpedance, grip strength, anthropometrics) with laboratory surveillance of ferritin, transferrin saturation, vitamin D, folate, B12, zinc, selenium, magnesium, phosphate, CRP, IL-6, and albumin.
- Cancer cachexia intervention engine (25-30 kcal/kg/day, 1.2-1.5 g protein/kg, EPA/DHA at 2 g/day or more, resistance exercise, appetite stimulation, anti-inflammatory modulation) with pharmacologic support (megestrol, olanzapine, short-term corticosteroids) and an enteral hierarchy: oral first, tube feeding second, parenteral nutrition only if gut failure persists beyond 7 days.
- Sickle-cell disease metabolic stabilization (folate 1 mg/day, vitamin D correction, hydration of 2-3 L/day, protein augmentation, electrolyte stabilization, renal protection, iron prohibition unless deficiency is proven, growth and development support).
- Cancer-plus-sickle-cell integrated metabolic logic managing conflicting requirements: protein need versus renal burden, hydration need versus heart failure, appetite stimulation versus thrombosis risk, iron avoidance versus anemia management, steroid use versus vaso-occlusive risk, and obesity versus sarcopenia masking, with adaptive algorithmic rebalancing.
- Obesity and body composition intelligence distinguishing sarcopenic obesity, cachectic obesity, metabolically unstable obesity, and low-muscle normal BMI, with cancer-specific and sickle-cell-specific physiologic implications.

**Methodological innovation**

- Social-physiologic fusion engine: insulin failure without refrigeration, antibiotic failure without food, and dialysis failure without transport are treated as clinical events, extending social-determinants logic into precision metabolic support.
- Conflict-domain arbitration: real-time algorithmic rebalancing of fluid status, cardiac reserve, renal filtration, oxygen delivery, metabolic demand, and inflammatory load when cancer and sickle-cell demands collide.
- Circadian meal scheduling: time-restricted feeding and chronobiology integration for metabolic optimization during cytotoxic therapy.

**Key performance indicators**

- Cachexia reversal rate
- Treatment interruption reduction
- Infection rate reduction
- Wound healing improvement
- Chemotherapy tolerance enhancement
- Vaso-occlusive crisis frequency reduction
- Quality-of-life trajectory
- Growth velocity (pediatric)

## Merits

- Governs nutritional intervention across oncology, sickle cell disease, chronic illness, and metabolic instability as a first-order therapeutic domain.
- Cachexia prevention and reversal designed to protect chemotherapy tolerance and reduce treatment interruption.
- Provides nutritional survivorship systems and resource-aware metabolic care that functions in constrained settings.
- Explicitly arbitrates the conflicting metabolic demands of coexisting cancer and sickle cell disease, a combination standard models cannot serve.
- Targets vaso-occlusive crisis frequency reduction through metabolic stabilization.
