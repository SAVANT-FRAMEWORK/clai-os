# P96: Surgical Oncology Margin & Morbidity Optimizer

## Abstract

Oncologic surgery is simultaneously a cytoreductive procedure, a physiologic stress test, an immunologic event, and a quality-of-life determinant. Margin adequacy alone is insufficient if achieved through catastrophic functional destruction, while organ preservation is inadequate if biologically incomplete. P96 continuously arbitrates between oncologic clearance, physiologic survivability, and post-treatment function through margin science, molecular imaging, frailty prediction, vascular risk modeling, and real-time intraoperative decision orchestration.

## Methodology

**Design objectives**

- Surgical intelligence core integrating histopathology, radiomics, molecular subtype, frailty scoring, cardiopulmonary reserve, inflammatory markers, nutritional status, and functional baseline into margin adequacy and morbidity probability outputs.
- Margin science framework with disease-specific definitions (breast: no ink on tumor; colorectal: at least 1 mm circumferential; pancreatic: R0 greater than 1 mm preferred; glioma: supramarginal beyond T1 enhancement; sarcoma: fascial plane integrity) and real-time intraoperative assessment via frozen section, fluorescence-guided surgery, confocal microscopy, and Raman spectroscopy.
- AI-augmented surgical imaging with fluorescence targeting (5-ALA in glioma, ICG perfusion mapping, tumor-specific fluorophores, sentinel node mapping) and machine-vision margin probability heatmaps.
- Morbidity and mortality prediction through composite risk architecture (ACS NSQIP, Charlson, Frailty Index, Nutritional Risk, VO2 max) predicting 30-day mortality, anastomotic leak, pulmonary complications, cardiac events, readmission, and functional decline.
- Sickle-cell disease surgical adaptation: preoperative optimization (hemoglobin target above 10 g/dL, exchange transfusion for major surgery, transcranial Doppler evaluation if prior stroke), intraoperative safeguards (avoidance of hypoxia, acidosis, hypothermia, dehydration), and postoperative acute chest syndrome prevention.

**Methodological innovation**

- Real-time margin arbitration: intraoperative fluorescence and machine vision provide immediate biological boundary assessment rather than waiting for postoperative pathological confirmation.
- Organ preservation intelligence: lumpectomy-versus-mastectomy oncoplastic eligibility, rectal cancer TEMS-versus-APR and watch-and-wait pathways, extremity sarcoma limb salvage versus amputation, and head and neck speech/swallow preservation with tracheostomy dependence modeling.
- Enhanced recovery integration: prehabilitation, goal-directed fluids, normothermia, opioid minimization, protective ventilation, early ambulation, and multimodal analgesia embedded into the surgical workflow.

**Key performance indicators**

- R0 resection rate by cancer type
- Margin assessment accuracy (frozen-versus-final correlation)
- 30-day morbidity and mortality prediction validity
- Organ preservation success rate
- Functional recovery trajectory
- Sickle-cell-specific perioperative complication reduction

**Integration**

- Upstream: P95 (Radiation Oncology Precision Planner) for neoadjuvant radiation sequencing; P91 (Genomic Variant Actionability Arbiter) for molecular subtype-informed resection extent.
- Downstream: P97 (CAR-T & Cell Therapy Manufacturing Guardian) for post-surgical cellular therapy timing; P108 (Rehabilitation & Survivorship Architect) for surgical late-effects and rehabilitation.

## Merits

- Transforms surgical oncology from retrospective pathology review into intraoperative, intelligence-guided intervention.
- Real-time margin analytics integrating Raman spectroscopy, fluorescence-guided surgery, and machine vision, with the design goal of reducing reoperation rates.
- Composite morbidity forecasting before incision, supporting honest consent and physiologic optimization.
- Structured arbitration between clearance and function protects quality of life without conceding oncologic completeness.
- Explicit sickle-cell perioperative safeguards address a population at elevated surgical risk.
