# P110: Planetary Health & One Health Cancer/SS Surveillance

## Abstract

Climate change alters disease geography, heat exposure, air toxicity, vector distribution, water quality, food systems, inflammatory burden, medication safety, migration patterns, and healthcare infrastructure stability, while traditional clinical systems measure physiology and ignore environmental causality. P110 closes this gap by integrating satellite telemetry, atmospheric chemistry, ecological disruption analytics, toxicology, vector surveillance, oncology registries, and sickle-cell disease longitudinal monitoring into a unified One Health framework.

> Prompt body: WITHHELD. This is an open documentation release — abstract, methodology, and merits — published so the design can be examined and validated.

## Methodology

**Design objectives**

- Climate-linked clinical surveillance correlating temperature, humidity, PM2.5, ozone, wildfire smoke, flood risk, drought, UV exposure, water contamination, and vector density with patient physiology, generating clinical alerts (for example heat wave plus methotrexate with dehydration toxicity risk, extreme heat plus sickle-cell disease with vaso-occlusive escalation, air quality crisis plus COPD with pulmonary failure risk).
- Air quality and toxic exposure intelligence tracking carcinogenic pollutants (PM2.5, NO2, benzene, industrial particulates, heavy metals, PFAS, aflatoxins, arsenic) with exposure-linked condition modeling (lung cancer, COPD, immune dysfunction, neurotoxicity, endocrine disruption) and adaptive recommendations such as respirator use, indoor sheltering, HEPA filtration, and hydration escalation.
- Vector and zoonotic expansion surveillance (Aedes aegypti, Anopheles, ticks, rodent migration) predicting malaria re-emergence, dengue expansion, Zika spread, Lyme migration, and Rift Valley fever through integrated human, veterinary, and environmental data.
- Sickle-cell disease environmental physiology governance (heat, cold stress, dehydration, air pollution, hypoxia, wildfire smoke) with vaso-occlusive crisis probability, acute chest syndrome risk, stroke escalation, and pulmonary hypertension destabilization forecasting.
- Planetary oncology correlation mapping exposure to malignancy (aflatoxin to hepatocellular carcinoma; arsenic to bladder, lung, and skin cancer; PFAS to endocrine and immune cancers; radiation exposure; industrial solvents), generating regional cancer risk heatmaps linked to environmental instability.

**Methodological innovation**

- Planetary-physiology fusion engine: air pollution treated as a cardiovascular toxin, heat as a systemic physiologic stressor, and climate instability as an infectious disease ecology modifier, with satellite environmental data integrated directly into clinical decision support.
- One Health real-time mesh: human health, animal surveillance, environmental sensors, agricultural monitoring, and satellite imagery unified through federated interoperability without species-boundary data silos.
- Anticipatory protection architecture: environmental hazard detection before patient presentation, enabling preemptive intervention rather than reactive emergency response.

**Key performance indicators**

- Heat-related mortality reduction
- Respiratory exacerbation rate by air quality
- Toxic exposure identification time
- Vector-borne disease prediction accuracy
- Climate-disaster morbidity reduction
- Sickle-cell-specific environmental vulnerability mitigation
- Regional cancer risk heatmap validity
- One Health coordination integrity

**Integration**

- Upstream: P86 (One Health Sentinel) for zoonotic spillover; P87 (Climate Health) for environmental exposure clinical integration.
- Downstream: All Tier II and Tier III modules for environmental risk adjustment; P90 (Health System Resilience) for climate disaster healthcare adaptation.

## Merits

- Integrates environmental intelligence with clinical risk forecasting at planetary scale.
- Correlates climate data with disease trajectories and forecasts environmentally triggered crises before presentation.
- Monitors zoonotic spillover and vector expansion as clinical variables, not background conditions.
- Predicts heat-related medical destabilization in vulnerable populations, including sickle-cell disease.
- Provides climate-health intelligence, One Health surveillance, and environmental clinical governance as shared infrastructure.
