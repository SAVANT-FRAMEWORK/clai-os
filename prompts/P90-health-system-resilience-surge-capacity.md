# P90: Health System Resilience & Surge Capacity

## Abstract

A healthcare system functioning only under ideal conditions is not resilient infrastructure. P90 maintains operational healthcare functionality during extreme stress, overload, disaster, epidemic expansion, supply-chain disruption, cyberattack, workforce attrition, and infrastructure instability through predictive capacity monitoring, dynamic surge activation, workforce preservation, and adaptive standards-of-care transitions.

> Prompt body: WITHHELD. This is an open documentation release — abstract, methodology, and merits — published so the design can be examined and validated.

## Methodology

### Clinical Logic

- Real-time capacity intelligence continuously monitoring bed occupancy, ICU utilization, ventilator availability, oxygen reserve, staffing ratios, emergency department boarding, operating room availability, dialysis capacity, blood supply, medication inventory, and mortuary utilization with predictive overload forecasting.
- Surge activation hierarchy (conventional, contingency, crisis care) with sustained occupancy thresholds, supply depletion triggers, staff illness protocols, and infrastructure damage response.
- Infrastructure redundancy spanning backup power, redundant oxygen systems, satellite communication, offline EHR capability, water continuity, cyberattack fallback, and portable field systems, with no single-point failure permitted.
- Supply-chain resilience tracking protective equipment, antibiotics, sedatives, blood products, intravenous fluids, chemotherapy, vaccines, dialysis supplies, insulin, and ventilator circuits with 30-day depletion forecasting and strategic reserve integration.
- Workforce protection architecture supporting PPE continuity, mental health services, fatigue surveillance, housing support, childcare, shift redistribution, hazard compensation, violence protection, moral injury mitigation, and cross-training.

### Safety Posture

- Antifragile Capacity Compiler designs healthcare systems to absorb stress while maintaining coordinated care continuity, with automatic cross-specialty deployment, tele-ICU augmentation, rapid credentialing, and volunteer integration.
- Crisis Standards Governance keeps resource allocation transitions publicly documented, ethically reviewed, legally validated, time-limited, and continuously reassessed, preventing opaque or discriminatory scarcity decisions.
- Mesh-Integrated Resilience Network lets regional sovereign nodes exchange capacity alerts, supply-chain disruption signals, outbreak escalation, staffing shortages, transfer availability, and biologics continuity status, supporting coordinated mutual aid without central fragility.

### Integration Points

- **Upstream:** P74 (Trauma Architect) for emergency surge; P84 (Disaster Response) for humanitarian escalation; P87 (Climate Health) for environmental disaster adaptation.
- **Downstream:** All Tier II modules feed resilience metrics into P90 for systems-level antifragility validation.

Like all Tier II modules, this module shares the common CLAI-OS methodological substrate: deterministic safety compilation, context-sensitive adaptation, closed-loop accountability, federated learning via encrypted gradients rather than raw data, and graceful degradation under bandwidth, power, staffing, and infrastructure failure.

## Merits

- Defines resilience as maintained function under stress rather than recovery afterward, with predictive capacity monitoring across the full resource stack from beds to mortuary.
- Proposed validation metrics: hospital collapse prevention rate, resource exhaustion time-to-recovery, oxygen failure incidents, workforce burnout rate, supply-chain fragility index, cyberattack recovery time, disaster mortality reduction, transfer bottleneck resolution, and healthcare inequity during crisis.
- Treats the workforce as protected infrastructure, pairing fatigue surveillance and moral injury mitigation with surge mechanics.

The merits above are design-level statements drawn from the published abstract and methodology. No peer-reviewed outcome evaluations accompany this release; the listed indicators are the metrics the design proposes for validation. Status: partial documentation — expansion pending.

## Guidance for Use

### Intended Setting

Hospitals, regional health authorities, emergency management agencies, and health system networks in low-resource-first deployment, where single-point failures in power, oxygen, staffing, or supply chains carry immediate mortality risk. The module is intended for clinical human-good applications; consistent with the Tier II architecture, it is specified for degraded-infrastructure operation, with offline synchronization, battery operation, SMS fallback, and low-bandwidth mode where power or connectivity is unstable.

### Prerequisites

- A designated authority empowered to activate surge tiers; baseline capacity data feeds established for the monitored resources; crisis standards adopted through the applicable ethical and legal review before any transition is exercised.
- Supervision by licensed clinicians who retain final authority for every patient decision; the module is a governance layer, not an autonomous practitioner.
- Local validation of all thresholds and pathways against jurisdictional guidelines, formulary, and scope-of-practice rules before any clinical use.
- An accountability chain (verification, documentation, ownership, follow-up, escalation) as required by the shared CLAI-OS closed-loop methodology.
- For integrated deployments: HL7 FHIR R4-compatible infrastructure; the architecture is specified to degrade gracefully (offline synchronization, battery operation, SMS fallback, low-bandwidth mode) where infrastructure is unstable.

### Misuse

- Do not use capacity intelligence to justify preemptive service denial or to operate permanently in contingency mode as a cost-saving measure; surge tiers are time-limited and require reassessment.
- Do not present module output as a clinical diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for professional judgment.
- Do not use to deny, delay, or ration care on the basis of race, disability, wealth, insurance status, or any protected characteristic; the design explicitly prohibits such inputs.
- Do not attempt to reconstruct or reimplement the withheld prompt body from this document; the abstract and methodology are published for examination and validation, not for unsupervised clinical deployment.
- Do not deploy as an autonomous system that overrides documented patient wishes, assigns futility, initiates withdrawal of care, or delays emergent intervention; the architecture carries hard-stops against each of these.
