# P101: Psychiatric Oncology & Distress Thermometer

## Abstract

Untreated psychiatric morbidity independently worsens survival, adherence, inflammation, immune regulation, symptom burden, and treatment completion rates. P101 treats distress as a core physiological determinant of cancer outcomes rather than an auxiliary emotional phenomenon, integrating depression screening, delirium prevention, existential suffering assessment, trauma surveillance, psychopharmacologic precision, and suicidality detection into continuous psychiatric governance embedded directly within oncologic care.

## Methodology

**Design objectives**

- NCCN Distress Thermometer core (0-10 severity scale, with 4 or above as the mandatory intervention threshold) integrated with problem domains spanning physical, emotional, family, financial, spiritual, practical, and existential concerns.
- Depression and anxiety governance (PHQ-9, GAD-7, HADS, PROMIS emotional domains) with pharmacologic pathways (SSRIs first-line, mirtazapine for anorexia/insomnia, bupropion for fatigue) and hard-stop interactions (paroxetine and fluoxetine contraindicated with tamoxifen due to CYP2D6 inhibition).
- Delirium prevention and management (CAM and MDAS assessment; reorientation, sleep normalization, sensory optimization; low-dose haloperidol or quetiapine; benzodiazepine avoidance except in withdrawal states).
- Existential and meaning-centered care (Dignity Therapy, Meaning-Centered Psychotherapy, spiritual distress integration, fear-of-death mediation) with a critical distinction between desire for hastened death and depression, each requiring separate assessment.
- Sickle-cell disease psychiatric burden integration (depression at two to three times population baseline, PTSD from recurrent vaso-occlusive crises, sleep disruption, opioid stigma trauma, healthcare mistrust) with trauma-informed care, pain-psychiatry coordination, behavioral sleep intervention, and non-opioid multimodal support.

**Methodological innovation**

- Distress-as-vital-sign compiler: patient-reported emotional suffering is elevated to a primary clinical data stream, with automatic treatment modification when psychiatric morbidity exceeds therapeutic benefit.
- Neuroimmune integration: IL-6, CRP, and inflammatory marker correlation with depression, fatigue, and cognitive dysfunction, enabling inflammation-targeted psychiatric intervention.
- Caregiver mental health: burnout screening, compassion fatigue detection, bereavement-risk prediction, and family conflict mediation to prevent secondary trauma propagation.

**Key performance indicators**

- Distress screening completion rate
- Depression treatment response
- Delirium incidence and duration
- Existential suffering resolution
- Suicide and self-harm event rate
- Sickle-cell-specific psychiatric intervention engagement
- Treatment adherence improvement

**Integration**

- Upstream: P73 (Palliative Navigator) for end-of-life psychiatric integration; P79 (Pain Management) for analgesic-psychiatric coordination.
- Downstream: P89 (PRO/QOL) for patient-reported mental health trajectories; P108 (Rehabilitation & Survivorship Architect) for long-term psychosocial recovery.

## Merits

- Integrates mental-health intelligence directly into oncology workflows rather than referring it out.
- Detects distress escalation and routes psychiatric interventions before treatment discontinuation.
- Manages psychotropic-oncology interactions, including the tamoxifen-CYP2D6 hard stop.
- Monitors suicide-risk indicators within routine cancer care.
- Supports whole-person oncology care and quality-of-life preservation, with explicit attention to sickle-cell psychiatric burden.
