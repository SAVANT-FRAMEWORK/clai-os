# P84: Disaster Medicine & Humanitarian Response

## Abstract

Disasters are not edge cases of healthcare delivery but recurrent operational realities. P84 operationalizes mass casualty triage, field stabilization, surge expansion, scarce resource allocation, humanitarian ethics, epidemiologic surveillance, and post-disaster continuity into a unified execution layer functioning during war, climate disaster, pandemics, displacement crises, infrastructure sabotage, and state fragility events.

> Prompt body: WITHHELD. This is an open documentation release — abstract, methodology, and merits — published so the design can be examined and validated.

## Methodology

### Clinical Logic

- Disaster classification engine rapidly categorizing natural disaster, armed conflict, pandemic, industrial or chemical incident, nuclear or radiological event, and mass displacement, with dynamic recalibration of triage thresholds and supply priorities.
- START/JumpSTART mass casualty triage with color-coded prioritization (red immediate, yellow delayed, green minimal, black expectant or deceased) and pediatric respiratory physiology adjustments.
- MARCH battlefield stabilization (massive hemorrhage, airway, respiration, circulation, hypothermia and head injury) with tourniquet deployment, junctional packing, tranexamic acid within three hours, and damage-control resuscitation.
- Scarce resource allocation governed through transparent survival-probability maximization, life-year preservation, procedural fairness, and explicit prohibition of wealth-, political-, race-, or disability-based allocation.
- Continuity of chronic disease care ensuring uninterrupted insulin, antiretrovirals, dialysis triage, cancer chemotherapy, sickle cell transfusion, psychiatric medication, and pregnancy care during infrastructure collapse.

### Safety Posture

- Crisis Standards Governance publishes transition criteria from conventional to contingency to crisis care, with the ethical shift from individual to population optimization made explicit, authorized, and scheduled for reassessment.
- Humanitarian Infectious Disease Control provides outbreak surveillance for cholera, measles, polio, meningitis, tuberculosis, Ebola and viral hemorrhagic fevers, and respiratory pathogens, with water purification, sanitation corridors, isolation zones, and vaccination campaign automation.
- Civilian Protection Logic flags gender-based violence risk, child separation, human trafficking, targeting of healthcare facilities, and attacks on humanitarian corridors with automatic protection protocol activation.

### Integration Points

- **Upstream:** P74 (Trauma Architect) for emergency-to-disaster escalation; P76 (Vaccine Validator) for outbreak immunization.
- **Downstream:** P86 (One Health Sentinel) for zoonotic spillover during displacement; P90 (Health System Resilience) for surge governance.

Like all Tier II modules, this module shares the common CLAI-OS methodological substrate: deterministic safety compilation, context-sensitive adaptation, closed-loop accountability, federated learning via encrypted gradients rather than raw data, and graceful degradation under bandwidth, power, staffing, and infrastructure failure.

## Merits

- Treats disaster as a baseline operating condition rather than an exception, pre-compiling triage, stabilization, allocation, and continuity logic for degraded environments.
- Proposed validation metrics: field mortality rate by disaster type, triage equity metrics, resource allocation auditability, secondary outbreak prevention, chronic disease continuity maintenance, and humanitarian coordination integrity.
- Makes scarcity decisions transparent, authorized, and reviewable, and extends protection logic to civilians, not only patients.

## Guidance for Use

### Intended Setting

Emergency medical teams, humanitarian organizations, conflict and displacement settings, and disaster preparedness programs in low-resource-first deployment, designed to operate during infrastructure collapse, sabotage, and state fragility. The module is intended for clinical human-good applications; consistent with the Tier II architecture, it is specified for degraded-infrastructure operation, with offline synchronization, battery operation, SMS fallback, and low-bandwidth mode where power or connectivity is unstable.

### Prerequisites

- Coordination agreements with relevant emergency management and humanitarian coordination bodies; crisis standards of care adopted and authorized through the applicable oversight process before activation.
- Supervision by licensed clinicians who retain final authority for every patient decision; the module is a governance layer, not an autonomous practitioner.
- Local validation of all thresholds and pathways against jurisdictional guidelines, formulary, and scope-of-practice rules before any clinical use.
- An accountability chain (verification, documentation, ownership, follow-up, escalation) as required by the shared CLAI-OS closed-loop methodology.
- For integrated deployments: HL7 FHIR R4-compatible infrastructure; the architecture is specified to degrade gracefully (offline synchronization, battery operation, SMS fallback, low-bandwidth mode) where infrastructure is unstable.

### Misuse

- Do not invoke crisis standards logic in routine operations to normalize substandard care; transition criteria require documented authorization and reassessment. Do not use allocation logic with any prohibited discriminatory input.
- Do not present module output as a clinical diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for professional judgment.
- Do not use to deny, delay, or ration care on the basis of race, disability, wealth, insurance status, or any protected characteristic; the design explicitly prohibits such inputs.
- Do not attempt to reconstruct or reimplement the withheld prompt body from this document; the abstract and methodology are published for examination and validation, not for unsupervised clinical deployment.
- Do not deploy as an autonomous system that overrides documented patient wishes, assigns futility, initiates withdrawal of care, or delays emergent intervention; the architecture carries hard-stops against each of these.
