# P73: Palliative Care & End-of-Life Dignity Navigator

## Abstract

Conventional palliative workflows activate late in disease progression, producing preventable terminal suffering, medically futile escalation, unmanaged pain crises, and family fragmentation. P73 treats palliative intervention as a longitudinal continuum beginning at serious illness diagnosis, integrating total suffering quantification, hospice eligibility intelligence, goals-of-care mediation, refractory symptom management, and bereavement risk stratification into a continuously updating clinical coordination layer.

> Prompt body: WITHHELD. This is an open documentation release — abstract, methodology, and merits — published so the design can be examined and validated.

## Methodology

### Clinical Logic

- Multidimensional suffering quantification (ESAS-r, IPOS, PHQ-9, GAD-7, CAM, Zarit scale) with dynamic trajectory analysis across physical, psychological, social, financial, existential, and caregiver burden domains.
- Hospice eligibility intelligence through continuous prognostic surveillance (PPS at or below 50 percent, ECOG at or above 3, recurrent hospitalization) to prevent late referral.
- Structured goals-of-care mediation using SPIKES, REMAP, and Serious Illness Conversation Guide frameworks with cultural and religious adaptation.
- Refractory symptom escalation governed via the WHO analgesic ladder, opioid rotation logic, methadone conversion, ketamine rescue, and proportional palliative sedation protocols.
- Family systems support with caregiver burden monitoring, bereavement risk stratification, child grief support, and legacy preservation.

### Safety Posture

- Total Suffering Quantification Engine recalculates composite suffering burden longitudinally rather than episodically, enabling preemptive intervention.
- Ethical Mediation Layer distinguishes killing from allowing natural death, proportional sedation from euthanasia, and comfort-focused treatment from abandonment through transparent reasoning pathways.
- AI Safety Governance hard-stops prevent AI override of documented patient wishes, independent futility assignment, or autonomous withdrawal initiation.

### Integration Points

- **Upstream:** P79 (Pain Management) for analgesic continuity; P80 (Ethics Navigator) for conflict mediation.
- **Downstream:** P89 (PRO/QOL) for patient-reported dignity metrics; P101 (Psychiatric Oncology) for existential distress.

Like all Tier II modules, this module shares the common CLAI-OS methodological substrate: deterministic safety compilation, context-sensitive adaptation, closed-loop accountability, federated learning via encrypted gradients rather than raw data, and graceful degradation under bandwidth, power, staffing, and infrastructure failure.

## Merits

- Repositions palliative care as a continuum from serious illness diagnosis, directly targeting late referral, unmanaged pain crises, futile escalation, and family fragmentation.
- Proposed validation metrics: symptom control trajectories, hospice timing index, ICU overutilization reduction, goal-concordant care rate, clinician moral injury scores, and bereavement complication rates.
- Encodes explicit ethical distinctions and AI hard-stops so that end-of-life decisions remain attributable to patients and clinicians, not to the system.

The merits above are design-level statements drawn from the published abstract and methodology. No peer-reviewed outcome evaluations accompany this release; the listed indicators are the metrics the design proposes for validation. Status: partial documentation — expansion pending.

## Guidance for Use

### Intended Setting

Oncology and supportive care services, hospice networks, intensive care units, geriatric services, and community palliative programs in low-resource-first deployment, where palliative intervention is most often absent or initiated only in the final days of life. The module is intended for clinical human-good applications; consistent with the Tier II architecture, it is specified for degraded-infrastructure operation, with offline synchronization, battery operation, SMS fallback, and low-bandwidth mode where power or connectivity is unstable.

### Prerequisites

- Clinicians trained in goals-of-care communication; local legal definitions of palliative sedation, advance directives, and withdrawal of treatment established before configuration.
- Supervision by licensed clinicians who retain final authority for every patient decision; the module is a governance layer, not an autonomous practitioner.
- Local validation of all thresholds and pathways against jurisdictional guidelines, formulary, and scope-of-practice rules before any clinical use.
- An accountability chain (verification, documentation, ownership, follow-up, escalation) as required by the shared CLAI-OS closed-loop methodology.
- For integrated deployments: HL7 FHIR R4-compatible infrastructure; the architecture is specified to degrade gracefully (offline synchronization, battery operation, SMS fallback, low-bandwidth mode) where infrastructure is unstable.

### Misuse

- Do not use prognostic or suffering scores to pressure patients toward comfort-only pathways or to justify unilateral treatment withdrawal.
- Do not present module output as a clinical diagnosis, prescription, or substitute for professional judgment.
- Do not use to deny, delay, or ration care on the basis of race, disability, wealth, insurance status, or any protected characteristic; the design explicitly prohibits such inputs.
- Do not attempt to reconstruct or reimplement the withheld prompt body from this document; the abstract and methodology are published for examination and validation, not for unsupervised clinical deployment.
- Do not deploy as an autonomous system that overrides documented patient wishes, assigns futility, initiates withdrawal of care, or delays emergent intervention; the architecture carries hard-stops against each of these.
