# P105: Data Sovereignty & Federated Learning Governance

## Abstract

Contemporary biomedical AI systems are structurally centralized, exporting genomic datasets from vulnerable regions into computational centers elsewhere and concentrating ownership, intellectual property, and economic value away from originating communities. P105 restructures this architecture through distributed computation designed so that models travel while protected data remains geographically sovereign.

## Methodology

**Design objectives**

- Federated learning infrastructure (PySyft, NVIDIA FLARE, TensorFlow Federated, Flower, OpenFL) with local institutional data retention, local model training, encrypted gradient transmission, secure aggregation, and global model update redistribution.
- Data residency enforcement with automatic jurisdictional mapping (for example GDPR-regulated data to EU-compliant infrastructure, PIPL-regulated data within China, HIPAA-regulated data within the United States, and African genomic data to African sovereign infrastructure), plus cross-border export restrictions, treaty validation, Material Transfer Agreement enforcement, and Indigenous governance review.
- Dynamic consent architecture via blockchain (Hyperledger Fabric) with real-time revocation, granular permission layers, secondary-use approval, community oversight, time-limited authorization, and smart-contract benefit-sharing.
- Genomic sovereignty protection for HBB haplotypes, HLA datasets, Indigenous genomic signatures, population-specific pharmacogenomics, and rare hereditary disease registries, with mandatory ethical review, community governance approval, local co-authorship enforcement, technology transfer obligations, and infrastructure reinvestment clauses.
- Differential privacy enforcement (epsilon below 1.0, delta below 10^-6) with homomorphic encryption, secure multiparty computation, trusted execution environments, and gradient clipping to prevent re-identification during collaborative learning.

**Methodological innovation**

- Extractive-colonialism termination: mathematical and structural guarantees designed to prevent genomic data export while enabling global-scale scientific collaboration.
- Smart-contract benefit-sharing: automatic economic value redistribution triggered by commercial licensing, model deployment, therapeutic discovery, and pharmaceutical utilization, without manual negotiation.
- Community governance integration: Indigenous and local community oversight embedded into computational infrastructure rather than treated as external advisory.

**Key performance indicators**

- Federated model accuracy versus centralized baseline
- Privacy guarantee maintenance (epsilon and delta compliance)
- Data residency violation rate (zero target)
- Consent revocation propagation time
- Benefit-sharing distribution accuracy
- Community governance participation rate
- Cross-border collaboration enablement

**Integration**

- Downstream: P110 (Planetary Health & One Health Cancer/SS Surveillance) for environmental data sovereignty; all Tier III modules for mesh-wide governance.

## Merits

- Establishes sovereign genomic AI infrastructure through federated learning, encrypted gradient exchange, and jurisdictional data residency enforcement.
- Advances African genomic sovereignty and anti-extractive biomedical infrastructure.
- Provides ethical AI governance primitives (consent, privacy, benefit-sharing) as structural features rather than compliance add-ons.
